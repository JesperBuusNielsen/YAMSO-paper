\subsection{Non-Committing Encryption}
\label{sec:nce}

Non-committing encryption (NCE)~\cite{CanettiEtAl1996AdaptiveMPC}
takes a public message length $\ell$, implicit in all calls. Write
\[
 (\pk,\sk)=\KeyGen(1^\lambda;\rho_R),\qquad
 c=\Enc(\pk,m;\rho_S),\qquad m'=\Dec(\sk,c;\delta).
\]
Here $m\in\bits^\ell$, $\rho_R$ and $\rho_S$ are the complete
key-generation and encryption tapes, and $\delta$ is the decryption
tape. Omitting a tape means sampling it independently. Keys and
ciphertexts have prescribed polynomial lengths. Decryption returns an $\ell$-bit string or $\bot$. An honest
encryption decrypts to $m$ except with negligible probability even when
$m$ is chosen after seeing $\pk$.

For one ciphertext per key, the key-first simulation~\cite{HemenwayEtAl2016QuasiOptimalNCE}
uses two calls
\[
 (\widetilde{\pk},\widetilde c,\SimState)\sample\SimNCE(1^\lambda),
 \qquad
 (\widetilde\rho_R,\widetilde\rho_S)\sample\ExplainNCE(\SimState,m).
\]
The first call fixes the pair. After seeing the key, the adversary
chooses $m$ and receives the prepared ciphertext. The simulator learns
$m$ on the first corruption. Except with negligible probability, replaying
its explained tapes regenerates $\widetilde{\pk}$ and encrypts $m$ to
$\widetilde c$. The key, ciphertext, and exposed tapes
together are computationally indistinguishable from a real execution, uniformly over
polynomially bounded public lengths (Appendix~\ref{app:nce}).

For application inputs, $\SNCE$ denotes full NCE, which permits exposure
of either or both tapes. For delivery, $\RNCE$ needs only the receiver's
explanation~\cite{JareckiLysyanskaya2000AdaptiveThreshold}.
Brakerski et al.\
\cite[full version, Theorems~23--24]{BrakerskiEtAl2020ConstantNCE}
achieve ciphertext length $O(\ell)+\poly(\lambda)$ under their DDH or
LWE assumptions.
