\subsection{Secret Sharing with Robust Reconstruction}
\label{sec:secret-sharing}

We use Shamir's threshold sharing~\cite[Section~2]{Shamir1979SecretSharing},
with error-and-erasure reconstruction as described by McEliece and
Sarwate~\cite{McElieceSarwate1981SecretSharing}.
The dealer generates a valid sharing; some recipients may subsequently
submit incorrect shares or no shares at all.  We do not require
verifiable secret sharing against a dishonest dealer.

\subsubsection{Syntax and reconstruction}
Let $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ and let $0\leq t<n$.  Let $\mathcal M$ be
a finite secret space and $\mathcal S$ a finite share space.
These public parameters are implicit inputs to all algorithms.
A scheme has two efficient algorithms:
\begin{description}
  \item[$\Share(s)\to\boldsymbol{s}=(s_1,\ldots,s_n)$.]
    Randomly share a secret $s\in\mathcal M$, producing one share
    $s_i\in\mathcal S$ for each indexed recipient.
  \item[$\Reconstruct(\widehat{\boldsymbol{s}})\to s'$.]
    Given $\widehat{\boldsymbol{s}}\in(\mathcal S\cup\{\bot\})^n$,
    deterministically return a secret in $\mathcal M$ or $\bot$.
    An input $\bot$ denotes a missing share.  Indices are fixed positions,
    not values that a recipient may change.
\end{description}
Perfect correctness requires reconstruction of $s$ from any $t+1$ or
more correct shares, with $\bot$ in the other positions.
For $n\geq3t+1$, we additionally require \emph{robust reconstruction}:
for every $s$, every $\boldsymbol{s}$ in the support of $\Share(s)$,
and every $\widehat{\boldsymbol{s}}$ that differs from
$\boldsymbol{s}$ in at most $t$ positions,
\[
  \Reconstruct(\widehat{\boldsymbol{s}})=s.
\]
A differing position may contain either an incorrect share or
$\bot$; the bound is on their total number.  In particular, the guarantee
does not depend on when the incorrect values were chosen.

\subsubsection{Perfect privacy}
For $b\in\bits$, the experiment
$\ShareGame^b_{\mathcal A}(n,t,\mathcal M,\mathcal S)$ gives the public parameters
to $\mathcal A$ and offers these commands:
\begin{description}
  \item[$\ShareSecret(s^{(0)},s^{(1)})$.]
    For $s^{(0)},s^{(1)}\in\mathcal M$, sample
    $\boldsymbol{s}\sample\Share(s^{(b)})$ and retain it privately.
    Return no shares.
  \item[$\ReadShare(i)$.]
    For $i\in[n]$, return $s_i$.  At most $t$ distinct positions may
    be queried; repeated queries return the same value.
  \item[$\Finish(b')$.]
    Terminate and output $b'\in\bits$.
\end{description}
There is at most one accepted $\ShareSecret$ command, and share queries
must follow it.  Malformed commands, a second $\ShareSecret$, and queries
exceeding the distinct-position limit return $\bot$ without changing
state.  Termination without an output bit is interpreted as $\Finish(0)$.

\begin{definition}[Perfect threshold privacy]
\label{def:sharing-privacy}
The scheme is perfectly $t$-private if, for every adversary
$\mathcal A$, including computationally unbounded adversaries,
\[
  \Pr[\ShareGame^0_{\mathcal A}(n,t,\mathcal M,\mathcal S)=1]
  =\Pr[\ShareGame^1_{\mathcal A}(n,t,\mathcal M,\mathcal S)=1].
\]
\end{definition}
The adversary may choose each queried index after seeing earlier shares.

\subsubsection{Shamir sharing}
Let $\mathbb F$ be a finite field with $|\mathbb F|>n$.
For this construction, $\mathcal M=\mathcal S=\mathbb F$.
Fix distinct nonzero points $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\in\mathbb F$.
To share $s$, choose $a_1,\ldots,a_t\sample\mathbb F$ independently and set
\[
  f(X)=s+\sum_{j=1}^t a_jX^j,
  \qquad s_i=f(\alpha_i)\quad(i\in[n]).
\]
Reconstruction uses Reed--Solomon decoding to recover a polynomial of
degree at most $t$ and returns its value at zero.  If $u$ positions are
missing, the decoder corrects up to
$\lfloor(n-t-1-u)/2\rfloor$ erroneous positions.  It returns $\bot$
if fewer than $t+1$ shares are present or no polynomial lies within this
decoding radius.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:shamir-sharing}
Shamir sharing has perfect correctness and perfect $t$-privacy.
It has robust reconstruction when $n\geq3t+1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Any $t+1$ correct evaluations determine $f$.  Any at most $t$ share
values are jointly uniform, independently of $s$, since their evaluation
constraints on the random coefficients have full rank.  Thus each new
share query within the privacy limit has a uniform answer even conditional
on the preceding queries and answers.
The evaluation code has minimum distance $n-t$, so error-and-erasure
decoding recovers $f$ whenever $2e+u<n-t$, where $e$ counts incorrect
shares and $u$ missing shares.  If $e+u\leq t$ and $n\geq3t+1$, this
inequality holds.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Completing partially exposed sharings}
The following standard property is called \emph{resampleability} by
Hazay, Venkitasubramaniam, and Weiss~
\cite[Section~2.2.1, Definition~2.6 and Remark~2.2]{HazayEtAl2025Resampling}.
Their Shamir resampling algorithm completes an exposed set of shares by
choosing further evaluations uniformly and interpolating with the secret.
We state the property in our notation and include the short interpolation
proof to make the distribution of the sharing randomness explicit.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional completion]
\label{lem:sharing-completion}
For Shamir sharing, there is an efficient algorithm
$\CompleteShares(I,(z_i)_{i\in I},s)$
such that, for every $I\subseteq[n]$ with $|I|\leq t$, every
$(z_i)_{i\in I}\in\mathbb F^{I}$, and every $s\in\mathbb F$, its output
has exactly the distribution of $\Share(s)$ conditioned on
$s_i=z_i$ for all $i\in I$.  It can also return the corresponding
sharing randomness $(a_1,\ldots,a_t)$ with its conditional distribution.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $t-|I|$ further indices outside $I$ and assign their evaluations
independent uniform values in $\mathbb F$.  Interpolate the unique
polynomial of degree at most $t$ through these evaluations, the fixed
evaluations $f(\alpha_i)=z_i$ for $i\in I$, and $f(0)=s$.
There are exactly $|\mathbb F|^{t-|I|}$ consistent polynomials, and the
new evaluation values select them bijectively.  The resulting polynomial
is therefore uniform among the consistent polynomials, exactly as in an
honest sharing conditioned on the fixed shares.  Output its evaluations
and, if requested, its nonconstant coefficients.
\end{proof}
For a fixed secret, the same completion statement holds conditional on
an adaptive share-query transcript: the choice of each index adds no
constraint on the unexposed shares beyond the answers already given.
Longer secrets are encoded as vectors over $\mathbb F$ and shared
componentwise with independent coefficients.
